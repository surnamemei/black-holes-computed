How to Photograph a Black Hole

Each pixel follows a light ray backwards through the Kerr spacetime of a spinning black hole, in units where its mass M = 1. The far side of the disk appears above and below the shadow because its light is bent around the hole. Turn Doppler off to see the symmetric picture, then raise the spin and watch the shadow flatten on one side.

I

The film

Five and a half minutes. Every frame was computed: the black holes by the ray tracer above, the telescope by the simulation below. Narration is synthesized speech; the music is synthesized too.

    II

    Build an Earth-sized telescope

    Each pair of telescopes measures one point of the image's Fourier transform. As the Earth turns, those points sweep arcs. Pick the stations and the hours, and see what image the gaps allow. The target is a simulated M87* in a 160 µas field, observed on 11 April 2017.

    Visibilities are gridded onto a 256 × 256 Fourier plane with uniform weighting and inverted with an FFT, as radio astronomers do. The reconstruction runs Högbom CLEAN, the classic algorithm that repeatedly removes the brightest copy of the dirty beam. The 2017 South Pole Telescope never sees M87, which stays below its horizon.

    III

    How it was made

    One session on one RTX 5090. The physics was checked against exact results before any frame of the film was rendered.

    • Ray tracer. Null geodesics of the Kerr metric are integrated backwards from the camera with the Hamiltonian in Mino time and adaptive Dormand–Prince steps, as a GPU kernel in single precision. A 1080p frame takes about 12 ms. In single precision a distant ray's impact parameter hides in the sixth digit of its radial momentum, so the null condition is re-imposed at every step.
    • Disk and color. Novikov–Thorne flux, Keplerian orbits, Doppler and gravitational redshift per pixel. A blackbody seen with redshift g is a blackbody at temperature gT; colors come from the CIE 1931 observer.
    • M87* model. Optically thin emission near the equator, where every crossing adds light, revealing the nested photon rings (89%, 10% and 0.7% of the flux).
    • Telescope. The 2017 EHT sites, Earth-rotation synthesis, radiometer noise, random atmospheric phases at every station, and an image solved from closure phases and closure amplitudes only, which those phases cannot touch.
    • Voice and music. Kokoro-82M speech, checked word by word by Whisper, which also confirmed every line stays intelligible over the music. The score is additive synthesis in NumPy.
