How to Hear Two Black Holes Collide

At 09:50:45 UTC on 14 September 2015, both LIGO detectors, 3,002 km apart in Washington and Louisiana, stretched and squeezed by about 4 × 10⁻¹⁸ m, a few hundredths of a proton's width. Two black holes had merged 1.3 billion light-years away. This page finds that signal again, starting from the raw recordings.

Hanford, whitened · energy

The data clip is two seconds of whitened Hanford strain: mostly hiss, then a rising "whoop" in the last fifth of a second. Its natural pitch, 35 to 250 Hz, is low enough that small speakers lose it, so by default it is shifted up 400 Hz with a single-sideband modulation that keeps the shape of the chirp.

I

Buried in noise

The raw strain is dominated by seismic and thermal noise at low frequencies, a hundred times larger than the signal. Dividing by the noise spectrum (whitening) and keeping 35–350 Hz reveals it.

Amplitude spectral densities come from 1,004 (Hanford) and 956 (Livingston) eight-second stretches of the hour around the event, median-averaged. The sharp spikes are the 60 Hz power line, its harmonics and the violin modes of the mirror suspensions. Where the signal curve rises above the noise, the detectors could hear it.

II

Searching with templates

General relativity predicts the exact waveform for each pair of masses. Sliding each prediction along the data and measuring the match (a matched filter, the optimal detector for a known signal in Gaussian noise) turns 1,066 templates into a map of signal-to-noise ratio.

III

Two detectors, one signal

Noise in Washington knows nothing about noise in Louisiana. A real wave must reach both within the 10 ms light-travel time, with the shape the detectors' orientations allow.

IV

How it was done

About 200 lines of NumPy and SciPy, plus one call to a waveform model. The full pipeline runs in two minutes on a desktop.

  • Data. 4,096 s of 4 kHz strain from each detector, from the Gravitational Wave Open Science Center.
  • Noise. Welch spectra from 8 s Hann segments, median-averaged (robust to glitches) and corrected for the median's bias; the 64 s around the event are excluded.
  • Templates. IMRPhenomD waveforms (inspiral, merger and ringdown) from LALSimulation, non-spinning, detector-frame masses 20–60 and 15–60 M☉ on a 1 M☉ grid.
  • Matched filter. Complex SNR from 30 to 1,024 Hz in the frequency domain; coincidence requires the two detectors' peaks within 10 ms.
  • Signal consistency. Allen's χ² test over 16 frequency bands of equal expected SNR, and the reweighted SNR used by the PyCBC search, which leaves signals alone and suppresses glitches.
  • Background. Livingston slid against Hanford in 3 s steps, 1,000 times, with the event removed: 44 days of fake coincidences.

This is a teaching pipeline, deliberately simple. It ignores spin, so its masses are biased toward equal values, and its background covers 44 days, not the hundreds of thousands of years of a full search. Even so, the signal stands far above everything the noise produced.
